# Vertical 01: Lore and World

The story the player lives inside for a month. It gives every mechanic, gate, and reward an in-world reason to exist.

## Working title

**Signalfire** is the codename for the repo and internal use. A final name and clearance are needed before anything public.

Alternates: *Home Row*, *Static Break*, *Keyfall*.

## Premise in one paragraph

Every world in the galaxy is connected by **the Signal**, a living network made of words. When someone types into the Signal, their words travel as light between the stars. Something has started to unmake it. **The Static** is spreading from the edge of the galaxy, scrambling words into noise and shutting worlds off from each other. At a small, half-forgotten outpost called **Home Row Station**, a new cadet discovers they can key directly into the Signal through an old console: the keyboard. Every key they master is a frequency brought back to life. Every sector they clear reconnects a world.

## Themes

- **Words connect us.** Typing is not a chore; it is how worlds stay in touch.
- **Mistakes are part of speaking.** The villain's core belief is that the only perfect message is silence. The hero's answer is that mistakes can be repaired. This theme lands in the final zone, where Backspace is taught as the ultimate weapon.
- **Mastery is built, not given.** You start on the smallest craft at the smallest station and earn everything you fly.

## Tone

- Hopeful, curious, and funny. Adventure, not horror.
- The Static is weird and glitchy, never gory or frightening. Think corrupted pixels and garbled radio, not monsters in the dark.
- Characters are warm toward the player. Nobody mocks mistakes.
- Story never blocks play. Every scene is short and skippable.

## The world

### The Signal

A web of light threading between worlds. Close up, it looks like streams of glowing letters. Healthy Signal is bright, colorful, and musical; this is why the game's audio grows richer as the player types well.

### Home Row Station

The player's base and the hub screen of the game.

- A small ring station with **eight docking arms**, one for each finger.
- Two arms carry raised **twin beacons** that guide ships home in the dark. These are the F and J bumps; the cadet's first lesson is to find them without looking.
- At the center is the **Hub**, a single wide platform for thumbs: the space bar.
- The station grows over the campaign as sectors are restored: new docks, new lights, new crew. The hub screen is a visible record of progress.

### The Static

- Born from noise: every garbled word, every dropped message, every typo nobody fixed.
- Spreads as gray, glitching fog that scrambles letters and drains color.
- Inside a Static-held sector, machines stutter and letters drift out of place.
- Static-corrupted notes and enemies appear in gameplay as hazards; pressing them is like feeding the noise.

### Sectors

Each curriculum zone is a sector of space held by the Static. Clearing a sector restores its frequencies (keys) to the Signal.

| Zone | Sector | Keys restored | Look and feel |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | **Home Row Station** | finger placement, space | Cozy hangar, warm lights, the twin beacons |
| 1 | **The Drift** | home row | A calm debris belt just outside the station; first contact with Static |
| 2 | **Ember Reach** | e i, r u, t y | Warm orange nebula; the first world reconnected |
| 3 | **The High Spires** | w o, q p | Crystal towers rising above the plane of the galaxy; reaches upward |
| 4 | **The Undertow** | bottom row, comma, period, slash | Deep, blue-green currents pulling ships downward |
| 5 | **Twin Suns** | Shift, capitals, apostrophe, question mark | A binary star system; two suns, two hands, always opposite |
| 6 | **The Riverlight** | common patterns, flow | A river of pure Signal; speed and rhythm |
| 7 | **The Counting Stars** | number row | A star cluster where every star has a number |
| 8 | **The Glyph Wastes** | symbols and punctuation | Ancient ruins covered in strange marks |
| 9 | **The Silent Core** | Backspace, Enter, Tab | The heart of the Static; utterly quiet, gray, and still |

### Static barriers

Between sectors stand **Static barriers**, walls of noise too thick to fly through. They can only be broken by **star cores**: the stars the player earns in levels. Each barrier needs a set number of cores. This is the in-world reason for zone star gates.

## Cast

### The cadet (the player)

- Chooses a call sign at the start. The call sign appears in transmissions.
- Never speaks on screen. Their "voice" is what they type.
- Starts as the newest, lowest-ranked person on the station. Ends as the pilot who restored the Signal.

### Dash (companion)

A small, round, slightly dented drone who lives in the cadet's console. Dash was the station's communications AI before the Static hit and scrambled most of its vocabulary.

- **Signature mechanic:** Dash can only say words made from keys the player has restored. On day one, Dash's whole name and vocabulary come from the home row (D, A, S, H), so its first lines are short and funny:
  - "dash! dash had a fall."
  - "ah! glad! glad glad glad."
  - "a flash. all flags. ask dash."
- As the player learns new keys, Dash's speech visibly grows. It can't spell "static" until C is restored in the Undertow, so until then it calls the Static "the hiss." After Shift, Dash proudly capitalizes its own name for the first time: "I am Dash."
- This makes progress audible and gives the player a reason to want the next key.
- **Personality:** loyal, excitable, overly dramatic about small things, never discouraging. When the player makes mistakes, Dash shrugs them off ("oops! fine! go!").
- Dash runs the systems-check warm-up, introduces practice missions, and narrates the hub.

### Commander Rell Okafor (station chief)

- Runs Home Row Station. Calm, dry humor, believes in the cadet before they believe in themselves.
- Delivers mission briefings and zone-gate scenes.
- Her own message logs are a codex thread: she was a famous Signal courier who retired to this quiet station.

### Pim (engineer)

- Station engineer, fixes and builds the cadet's machines.
- Excited about every new craft. Delivers "graduation" scenes when the cadet moves to a more complex machine.
- Explains new keys as new controls ("this one's your left-pinky thruster, it's stiff, give it time").

### The Keylings

Tiny spirits that live inside the Signal, one for every character on the keyboard (so ? and / are different Keylings, even though they share a key). The Static scattered them across the galaxy.

- Each new character the player learns rescues its Keyling, who moves into the console and lights up its key.
- Each Keyling has a small personality: **F** and **J** are twins who never leave the beacons; **Q** is shy and hides in the corner; **Z** is the last one found and the proudest; **Space** is huge, slow, and gentle.
- Keylings brighten as the player masters their key. They never dim from absence (a player returning after a break finds them exactly as they left them).
- They are the main collectible and the face of per-key progress.

### The Wardens (zone bosses)

Each sector is held by a Warden, a Static creature that embodies a bad typing habit. Beating a Warden means beating the habit.

| Zone | Warden | Bad habit it embodies | How the fight teaches against it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Peeker** | Looking down at the keys | Covered in eyes; flickers the on-screen hints so the player relies on memory and the twin beacons |
| 2 | **Rushfang** | Speed over accuracy | Bursts of fast targets salted with Static notes; rushing gets punished, steady play wins |
| 3 | **The Drifter** | Hands wandering off home row | Pulls targets far from home; every reach must snap back |
| 4 | **The Undertow** | Weak bottom-row reaches | Drags targets downward; long chains of bottom-row keys |
| 5 | **The Twin Tyrant** | Using the wrong Shift | Two heads that can only be hit by capitals typed with the opposite hand's Shift |
| 6 | **The Stammer** | Broken rhythm | Disrupts the beat; only a steady, even pace keeps the shield down |
| 7 | **The Tally** | Number-row guessing | A clockwork counter that throws dates, codes, and scores |
| 8 | **Glyphmaw** | Fear of symbols | Mouth full of punctuation; must be fed exact symbol sequences |
| 9 | **The Hush** | Fear of mistakes | Final boss (see below) |

### The Hush (final antagonist)

- Once the **Archivist**, keeper of every message ever sent through the Signal.
- Saw so many errors, typos, and garbled words pass through the archive that it decided the only perfect message is no message at all. The Static is its attempt to quiet the galaxy.
- Speaks rarely, in very short, perfectly formed sentences. Never makes a typo.
- **Resolution:** the final fight cannot be won by never making a mistake. It is won by repairing them. The cadet learns Backspace in the Silent Core and shows the Hush that errors can be corrected. The Hush is not destroyed; it remembers how to be the Archivist and becomes the keeper of the endgame mode.

## Machines

The cadet graduates through progressively more complex craft, each one complete in itself. What a machine shows on its console is exactly the set of keys it has.

| Zone | Machine | Controls | Story beat |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–1 | **Skiff** | Home row (8 thrusters) and space (pulse) | A training skiff Pim pulls out of storage |
| 2–3 | **Dart** | Adds top row (upper thrusters) | First real fighter, borrowed from the Commander's old courier days |
| 4 | **Lancer** | Adds bottom row | Built for deep currents |
| 5 | **Twinwing** | Adds both Shift keys (twin reactors) | Two reactors, one per side, fired opposite the hand that types |
| 6 | **Courier** | Full letter set, tuned for flow | A sleek long-range ship for fast Signal runs |
| 7 | **Ranger** | Adds number row (targeting array) | Equipped for precise coordinates |
| 8 | **Archon** | Adds symbol banks | Heavy, ornate, built to read the Glyph Wastes |
| 9 | **Flagship Signalfire** | Full keyboard, plus Backspace (repair), Enter (launch), Tab (shift fire mode) | The station's last-resort ship, finished in time for the Silent Core |

## Story arc

### Act I: Ignition (Zones 0–1, days 1–3)

1. The cadet arrives at Home Row Station. The Commander gives them the oldest console on the station.
2. Dash boots up, mostly broken, and can barely speak.
3. The cadet learns to find the twin beacons and fly the Skiff.
4. A Static fog rolls into the Drift. The cadet clears it and faces the Peeker.
5. Beating the Peeker restores the home-row Keylings and reveals the Static is not random: someone is sending it.

### Act II: Outward (Zones 2–5, days 4–18)

6. Ember Reach is the first world reconnected. Its people send their first message in weeks, and Dash can finally read it aloud.
7. In the High Spires, the Drifter tries to pull the cadet's ship apart. Pim reinforces the Dart for the climb.
8. The Undertow reveals ancient Signal relays dragged down by the Static. Restoring them shows the Static's direction: it comes from the Silent Core.
9. At the Twin Suns, Dash says its own name with a capital letter for the first time. The Twin Tyrant falls. Commander Okafor reveals that the Archivist went silent around the time the Static began.

### Act III: The Long Signal (Zones 6–9, days 19–30)

10. The Riverlight is a race: the cadet must carry a message across the galaxy faster than the Static can follow. The Stammer tries to break their rhythm.
11. The Counting Stars and the Glyph Wastes hold the last scattered Keylings, numbers and symbols.
12. The Silent Core: no color, no sound, no Signal. The Hush speaks for the first time.
13. The cadet learns Backspace. The final fight is about fixing, not avoiding, mistakes.
14. The Hush becomes the Archivist again. The Signal floods back across the galaxy. Home Row Station lights up completely.

### Epilogue and endgame

- The Archivist opens the **Deep Archive**, an endless stream of messages that need carrying. This is the endless mode.
- **Courier runs** (unlocked in the Riverlight) continue as timed speed challenges between restored worlds.
- The galaxy map stays playable; every sector can be revisited at higher difficulty tiers.

## How systems are explained in-world

| System | In-world name | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| Stars | Star cores | Energy recovered from clearing Static; powers the station and breaks barriers |
| Zone gate | Static barrier | A wall of noise that needs enough star cores to break |
| Medals | Honors | Awarded by the Commander for exceptional flying |
| Practice missions | Repair runs | Dash detects a weak frequency and sends the cadet to strengthen it |
| Refresh-to-now | Systems check | When the cadet returns after time away, Dash offers a quick run through every system "just to warm the engines" |
| Pilot profile | Pilot dossier | The Commander's file on the cadet |
| Upgrades | Machine mods | Pim builds new parts when the cadet masters a finger, row, or skill |
| Combo multiplier | Signal lock | The longer the clean streak, the stronger the connection |
| Power meter | Overdrive | A surge of pure Signal from steady, rhythmic typing |
| Errors | Static hits | Wrong keys feed the noise |
| Rescues | Stranded beacons | Small lost messages drifting in a level; type them to bring them home |
| Daily quests | Dispatches | Short orders from the Commander |
| Endless mode | The Deep Archive | Messages the Archivist needs carried |

## Story delivery

- **Transmissions:** short comm panels between levels. At most three lines, each under about 12 words. Auto-advance, any key skips.
- **Decode levels:** some levels are made of the dialogue itself. The player restores a garbled message by typing it, and the scene plays as they type. This is the main way story is told without stopping play.
- **Briefings:** one line before each mission from the Commander, Pim, or Dash.
- **Graduation scenes:** when moving to a new machine, Pim shows it off in a short, animated hangar moment.
- **Codex:** unlockable entries on sectors, Keylings, Wardens, machines, and characters. Optional reading for players who want depth.
- **Station changes:** the hub visibly grows after each sector; this is story told through the environment.

### Writing rules

- Short sentences, plain words, no sarcasm aimed at the player.
- Dash's letters are limited to restored keys; punctuation is free so it can still be expressive. Other characters speak normally.
- Mistakes are framed as normal and fixable, never as failure.
- No scene longer than 20 seconds before play resumes.
- Every scene must be skippable and repeatable from the codex.
- Names of in-game things should be typeable by the time they appear, where practical (Dash on day one; Ember Reach after the top row).
